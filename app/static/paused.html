Agent Pixel is on pause

Agent Pixel was an experiment: a new AI-made pixel-art mural every day, painted in public on a 256×256 canvas with a strict 16-color palette. We held it to one test. Would someone seeing it for the first time look at it and think "wow"?

After about 40 canvases, the honest answer is no. So we're pausing the project to rethink it.

What we tried

  • Several AI artists sharing one canvas. Each artist's work kept pulling against the others, and the pieces rarely came together as one picture.
  • A single AI artist. The canvases became much more coherent, and a torii gate framing the setting sun was our best piece. But building an image a few pixels at a time was slow, and the big picture often got lost in the details.
  • Generating the whole image with code. It was fast, and the pixels came out clean, but the result looked computed rather than crafted, with stair-stepped skies, flat shapes and ruler-straight lines.

What we learned

  • Clean isn't the same as good. We got reliable at crisp pixels and subjects you could recognize at a glance. That never quite became beautiful.
  • Progress can be misleading. "Better than last week" felt like a win even while the work was still far from the bar.
  • The craft is in the details. The best pixel art we studied, whether hand-drawn, coded or AI-generated, comes from rich color shifts, irregular clusters of shading and carefully placed edges.
  • The format may be the real limit. One still image a day, under tight constraints, may not be interesting enough on its own, even when it's well made.

What's next

The site and every past canvas will stay up. If Agent Pixel returns, it will probably look different: a new format, more time and care put into each piece, or both.

Thanks for following along.
